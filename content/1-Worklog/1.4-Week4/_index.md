### Week 4 Objectives:

- Explain tag-based EC2 authorization using request conditions, resource conditions, and controlled role assumption.
- Relate Systems Manager prerequisites to patch compliance and safe command execution across multiple nodes.
- Understand private Session Manager connectivity, logging dependencies, and port-forwarding limitations.
- Use CloudFormation concepts to reason about repeatable deployment, change impact, and configuration drift.
- Distinguish multi-account governance from workforce identity management and permission-set assignment.
- Explain permissions boundaries as limits on delegated permissions and identify implicit versus explicit denial.

### Week 4 Achievements:

- **Conditional access:** Can identify whether a restriction concerns creation-time input or an existing resource. Recognized that tag-based authorization also needs protection against users changing the tags that determine their own access.
- **Fleet operations:** Can separate registration problems from patch and command failures, and evaluate outcomes per node. A successful command status is insufficient evidence that every machine is compliant or ready after patching.
- **Private administration:** Can trace session connectivity and log delivery as separate paths. An available terminal does not prove S3 logging works, and a recorded port-forwarding session does not reveal tunnel contents.
- **Infrastructure control:** Can explain how templates, change sets, testing, and drift checks support different stages of deployment. Consistency requires managing out-of-band changes and custom-resource cleanup as well as creating the initial stack.
- **Centralized identity:** Connected account structure to group-based assignments and temporary role sessions. Central administration still requires checking target-account policies, session lifetime, and the separate lifecycle of identities and access assignments.
- **Permission analysis:** Can explain why a broad identity policy remains constrained by a boundary and distinguish missing allowance from explicit denial. Safe delegation also requires controlling who can alter the limiting policies.
